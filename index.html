How it works

It tells you
what to do next.

Choosing what to study well requires knowing what you are missing, which is the thing you do not have yet. So it is not a library you browse. Open any section and one step is already waiting, with one button under it.

  1. 01 It picks

    Walk the line, then play it from memory, then handle him leaving the book. That order is the argument: you cannot recall what you have not seen, and off-book is meaningless until the book is automatic.

  2. 02 You do it

    One step, one filled button. Everything else on the screen is quieter on purpose, so there is never a moment of deciding between six things that all look equally important.

  3. 03 The next one appears

    Finish it and the following step is already there — and when a tab runs out, it hands you to the one that has not. You are never returned to a menu.

Try it here

Before you move,
decide.

This is how every endgame in the app opens. Not “find the move” — something harder, and the thing that actually decides these games.

You are White. It is your move.

Can this be won?

Not “can I find a good move”. Can this position be won at all, against the best defence there is?

The method: Your king goes first, in front of the pawn. Push the pawn first and you draw a game you were winning.

  1. Never push the pawn while your king is behind it — it has nothing to escort it with.
  2. Walk the king up until one square separates the kings and it is his move. That is the opposition, and it means he has to give way.
  3. He steps aside; you step forward. Repeat.
  4. Only now does the pawn move, and it moves with the king in front of it the whole way.

Why it sticks

Everything comes back
before you forget it.

The answer to “I used it once and then it was redundant”. Everything you finish returns on a schedule, and it arrives at the point you are about to lose it — which is the moment practising it is worth anything at all.

Expanding intervals

One day, three, a week, then longer. Get one wrong and it drops back two steps, not to zero — landing on nothing is discouraging, and it is not what the research says either.

The struggle is the mechanism

An item is served when it is nearly forgotten, not while it is still fresh. Easy recall builds nothing. That difficulty is not a side effect of the method; it is the method.

Questions

The ones people
actually ask.

What do I actually do in it?

You open it and it tells you the next step. Walk an opening line, play it back from memory, handle the replies that leave the book, read a middlegame position, or play an endgame out against best defence. There is no menu to choose from unless you go looking for one.

How is this different from puzzles?

Puzzles tell you a tactic exists before you look, which is the one thing a real game never does. Most positions contain nothing at all, and the skill that decides those games is knowing what to do when there is nothing to find. That is what the middlegame section trains, on positions verified to hold no tactic.

Is the content actually correct?

Every move, branch, position and endgame result was checked against Stockfish before it shipped, and anything the engine disagreed with was dropped rather than published. 995 moves across the whole library, none of them wrong. The checking refused a lot of our own writing, and that writing is not in the app.

Will I just forget it all again?

That is what the review schedule is for. Everything you finish comes back at one day, three, a week, then longer — arriving at the point you are about to forget it, which is when practising it is worth anything. Get one wrong and it drops back two steps rather than to zero.

What rating is this for?

Roughly 300 to 1400. It is built for the point where you know how the pieces move but not what to do with them, and where every game ends the same three ways.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. All three sections, the full board, and your progress follows the account rather than the device.

Can I cancel any time?
